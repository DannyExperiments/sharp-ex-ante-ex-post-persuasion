# Q489 source and priority assessment

**Verdict.** The exact worst-case persuasion constant and the Lipschitz-class supremum were not located in the checked sources. The finite-support principle, discounted code tree, binary extremizers and the M=2 witness have earlier sources. The contribution is to remove the barycenter equations inside each auxiliary pool while preserving their overall mixture, apply a symmetrized multiway inequality, and match it with the tree utility on a prior-based sharp instance. Moderate confidence in the bounded priority search.

## Exact original question and earlier sharpness

Yakov Babichenko, Inbal Talgam-Cohen and Konstantin Zabarnyi, *Bayesian Persuasion under Ex Ante and Ex Post Constraints*, Proceedings of AAAI 35(6), 5127–5134 (2021), DOI 10.1609/aaai.v35i6.16648. The main theorem and open question appear in published §5, immediately before Proposition 5.4, and in arXiv v2 §5, immediately before Proposition 5.5. The newest arXiv version is v2, 11 April 2021. [AAAI primary PDF](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/16648/16648-13-20142-1-2-20210518.pdf#page=6) · [ArXiv v2, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03272v2#S5) · [Catalog Q489](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/game_theory/algorithmic-game-theory-learning-network-games.md#q489).

Section 2 takes a finite state space and a prior, with Bayes-plausible signals represented as probability laws on posteriors whose mean is the prior. Assumption 5.2 is the two-point multiplicative Jensen relaxation for the sender's nonnegative posterior utility. The source permits upper semicontinuity and assumes continuous constraints. Theorem 5.3 uses convex constraint functions and sequentially pools once per constraint, giving an M^m universal factor. The general-m tightness question refers to the optimum gap, as distinguished in the next proposition.

- Published Proposition 5.4(a) / arXiv Proposition 5.5(i) shows that the **sequential conversion analysis** can lose 2^m at M=2. For more than one constraint its example admits a different ex-post scheme with a smaller loss. This is not an optimum gap of 2^m.
- Published Proposition 5.4(b) / arXiv Proposition 5.5(ii) gives the exact optimum factor M for one constraint.
- ArXiv Proposition 5.5(iii) and Appendix E separately give an actual gap of m+1 at M=2. They use the same n=m+1 uniform states and coordinate caps p_i≤1/n, with sender utility max_i p_i. The example supplies a lower bound for general m, and not a matching universal upper bound. At beta=1/2 the binary discount sum is one by Kraft equality, so the new utility agrees with the older example at the uniform prior and at point masses, which are the only posteriors needed to compute its gap. [Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03272v2#A5).

The theorem here is stated in the posterior-utility formulation used in source §5. If one wants an explicit compact receiver action model, draw an action a from the simplex and give receiver payoff 2a_i−||a||² in state i. The unique best response to posterior p is a=p, so a state-independent sender payoff u(a) implements exactly the desired u(p). This does not claim tightness for a specific auction or finite receiver action family.

## Support and coding ingredients

- Laura Doval and Vasiliki Skreta, [*Constrained Information Design*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03588), supply the finite-support reduction noted in Babichenko et al. Fact 3.1. They preserve the prior along with the ex-ante constraints, so their support count depends also on the number of states. Standard extreme-point support bounds for moment sets also apply to the finite weight polytope. The proof uses this elementary support argument locally for weights **without** an imposed barycenter, then preserves the original prior by mixing the pooled posteriors. See [Winkler, *Extreme Points of Moment Sets*](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.13.4.581) and [Babichenko et al., Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03272v2#A1).
- Michael B. Baer, [*Optimal Prefix Codes for Infinite Alphabets with Nonlinear Costs*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0511003), §II, explicitly states that for a finite distribution and an exponential base beta≤1/2, the optimal binary code can be truncated unary. This is the comb tree. At beta=1/2 uniform weights give the same value for all full trees. Baer's [*Redundancy-Related Bounds on Generalized Huffman Codes*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0702059), exponential-average analysis, describes the finite exponential Huffman merge of two weights into beta times their sum and attributes its development to Humblet and Parker among others. For 0<beta<1 the minimization of the logarithm to base beta is equivalent to maximizing the discounted sum. Campbell's usual positive-exponent exponential-length minimization uses a different sign of the objective. See [Parker, *Conditions for Optimality of the Huffman Algorithm*](https://doi.org/10.1137/0209035).
- With uniform weights the balanced case for beta>1/2 follows by the elementary deepest-siblings versus shallowest-leaf exchange. It is the uniform special case of the established finite exponential prefix-code optimization, so the manuscript does not claim new tree extremizers. Baer treats exponential penalties among existing D-ary prefix-code methods in [*Twenty (or so) Questions: D-ary Length-Bounded Prefix Coding*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0602085), §II. The same full D-ary exchange gives threshold beta=1/D. When the leaf count is compatible with a full D-ary tree, the discounted Huffman merge of D equal weights is below one leaf weight for beta<1/D, so successive merges can form the D-ary comb. At beta=1/D the full-tree Kraft sum is one. These are coding facts; the persuasion proof uses the original binary mixture assumption.

The Lipschitz supplement keeps the same multiplicative Jensen constant by averaging local suprema over radii. It supplies a quantitative approach to the sharp factor, with no assertion that a Lipschitz maximizer always exists. Targeted searches by the original open question, theorem and exact terminology, nearby constrained-information papers, and coding analogues found no earlier combined result at this scope. No priority claim is made.
